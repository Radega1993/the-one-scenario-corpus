## Scenario C2 — C2_ExamDay_LongStays

### 1. Overview

- **Scenario ID:** C2  
- **Name:** C2_ExamDay_LongStays  
- **Family:** Campus  
- **Settings file:** `corpus_v1/02_campus/C2_ExamDay_LongStays.settings`

**Objective**

This scenario models **exam-day dynamics on a campus**: few sessions with **long stays** in exam rooms (low mobility, long wait times) and slow movement between locations. Students remain in place for 10–30 minutes, then move slowly to the next venue. It provides a **low-mobility, high-pause** campus variant compared with the class-change baseline (C1), stressing store-carry-forward and limited contact opportunities during exam windows.

### 3. Mobility model

C2 uses **RandomWaypoint** over a 750×550 m area with **exam-day levers**:

- **Speed** 0.2–0.5 m/s: very slow movement (e.g. walking in corridors, entering/leaving exam rooms).  
- **Wait time** 600–1800 s (10–30 min): long stays in one place, mimicking time spent in an exam session.  
- Nodes choose new waypoints uniformly; the long pauses dominate the epoch, so mobility is **low** compared with C1.

Single host group, 48 homogeneous nodes, no map or hotspots.

**DTN implication**

Low speed and long pauses **reduce contact opportunities** and **increase reliance on store-carry-forward**. Path diversity is limited; messages created during a long stay may only be forwarded when nodes move again. This stresses protocols that assume more frequent mixing.

### 4. Traffic pattern

Traffic is generated by a **single MessageEventGenerator** (`Events1`):

- **Interval**: `Events1.interval = 60, 120` → mean ≈ **90 s** between messages per source (higher rate than C1).  
- **Size**: `Events1.size = 50k, 150k` → mean ≈ **100 kB**.  
- **Sources/destinations**: `Events1.hosts = 0, 48` (all nodes can participate; random destinations).

So **traffic rate is higher** while **mobility is lower**: more messages are offered during periods when contacts are relatively scarce, increasing contention and dependency on buffering and TTL.

**DTN implication**

The combination of **high offered load** (90 s mean interval) and **sparse contact opportunities** (long stays, slow movement) tends to increase buffer pressure and drop risk, especially with a **short TTL (60)**. Delivery depends strongly on whether messages can be forwarded in the next mobility window.

### 5. Expected network behavior

- **Sparse contact opportunities:** long pauses and slow speed reduce mixing; many contacts occur only when nodes move between “sessions”.  
- **Moderate-to-low delivery** is plausible: limited forwarding windows and short TTL can cause messages to expire before reaching the destination.  
- **Overhead** may be moderate if replication happens mainly during the brief mixing phases.  
- **Latency** can be high when messages are created during a long stay and must wait for the next movement phase.

### 6. Role in the corpus

This scenario is included as the **exam-day campus variant** with:

- low mobility (slow speed, long wait times),
- higher traffic rate than C1 (90 s vs ~50 min mean interval),
- short TTL (60) and same family (Campus, RandomWaypoint, free space).

It is used to compare **low-mobility, high-pause** behavior against the class-change baseline (C1) and other campus scenarios (hackathon, library, stadium), and to stress store-carry-forward and buffer/TTL sensitivity.

### 7. Distinguishing characteristics

- **Very long wait times** (10–30 min) and **very low speed** (0.2–0.5 m/s): exam-room semantics.  
- **Short TTL (60)** relative to the long pauses: messages must be delivered within a limited time.  
- **Single traffic stream** with **higher rate** than C1, increasing load during sparse contacts.  
- Same **free-space RandomWaypoint** and **single group** as C1, but with opposite stress: temporal structure dominated by long stays rather than periodic waves.
