## Scenario C6 — C6_EmergencyDrill_Evacuation

### 1. Overview

- **Scenario ID:** C6  
- **Name:** C6_EmergencyDrill_Evacuation  
- **Family:** Campus  
- **Settings file:** `corpus_v1/02_campus/C6_EmergencyDrill_Evacuation.settings`

**Objective**

This scenario models an **emergency drill / evacuation** with **fast, directional movement toward an exit**. Unlike the other campus scenarios that use free-space RandomWaypoint, this one uses **LinearMovement** so the crowd moves along a corridor-like line from an entrance side to a far-end target, producing short-lived, directionally structured contacts during the evacuation.

### 3. Mobility model

C8 uses **LinearMovement** in a 500×400 m world:

- Nodes move on a line from `startLocation = (50,200)` toward `endLocation = (450,200)`.  
- `targetType = 1` biases movement toward the far end (evacuation target).  
- Speeds are high (2–4 m/s) and pauses are near-zero (0–10 s), so the population rapidly “flows” toward the exit.
- Scenario duration is **7,200 s (2 h)**.

**DTN implication**

Directional motion creates **transient, structured contacts**: nodes co-move, form short-lived neighborhood overlaps, and then quickly separate. Compared to random mixing, path diversity can be lower and forwarding windows can be brief, but contact durations can be sufficient for short messages during co-movement.

### 4. Traffic pattern

Traffic is generated by a single `MessageEventGenerator` (`Events1`):

- **Interval**: 10–30 s (mean ≈ 20 s).  
- **Size**: 10k–50k (mean ≈ 30 kB).  
- **Sources/destinations**: random among 80 nodes.

**DTN implication**

With fast motion and short pauses, forwarding depends on whether contacts form during the evacuation flow. The smaller message sizes improve deliverability under short contact opportunities; the long TTL makes expiry less dominant than mobility structure.

### 5. Expected network behavior

- **Short-lived forwarding opportunities:** contacts are shaped by the evacuation flow rather than steady mixing.  
- **Moderate delivery is plausible:** co-movement can enable transfers, but directional separation can reduce multi-hop diversity.  
- **High overhead is possible under Epidemic:** flooding can still replicate heavily when clusters of co-moving nodes meet.  
- **Latency can be low for messages created early:** early injections may reach many nodes during the flow; later injections may have fewer opportunities.

### 6. Role in the corpus

This scenario is included as the **directional evacuation** campus case:

- introduces **LinearMovement** (mm_Linear=1), unlike the other campus scenarios,
- stresses fast, transient contacts under high speed,
- serves as a structural contrast to RandomWaypoint campus baselines (C1–C4, C2/C3/C7).

### 7. Distinguishing characteristics

- **LinearMovement evacuation flow** (start→end, `targetType=1`) instead of free-space random mixing.  
- **Very high speed** (2–4 m/s) and **near-zero pauses** (0–10 s).  
- **Smaller messages** (10–50 kB) at relatively high rate (10–30 s).  
- Short scenario (2 h) modeling an evacuation drill window.
