### 3. Mobility model

Three ClusterMovement groups, 12 nodes each, clusterRange 350 m.

**DTN implication**

Rural scenarios stress **delay tolerance** and **sparse connectivity**: contacts are infrequent, path diversity is limited, and store-carry-forward dominates. Extreme range (R6, R9) or extreme speed (R11, R12) levers alter contact dynamics significantly.

### 4. Traffic pattern

Traffic is generated by MessageEventGenerator(s) with parameters tuned to the rural narrative (low rate for wildlife, small payloads for rescue, etc.).

**DTN implication**

Low contact rates and long TTL (e.g. R3) favour patience; short TTL (R5) stresses time-critical delivery. Buffer stress (R7) and intermittent power (R8) add resource constraints.

### 5. Expected network behavior

- Sparse contacts, high store-carry-forward dependency.  
- Delivery sensitive to range, speed, and buffer.  
- Overhead can spike when flooding meets rare contact windows.  
- Latency typically high unless range/speed are extreme.

### 6. Role in the corpus

This scenario represents a **rural communication regime** contributing diversity in connectivity, resource stress, and delay tolerance relative to Urban/Campus/Disaster baselines.

### 7. Distinguishing characteristics

- Tests protocol behaviour under low connectivity and resource constraints.
