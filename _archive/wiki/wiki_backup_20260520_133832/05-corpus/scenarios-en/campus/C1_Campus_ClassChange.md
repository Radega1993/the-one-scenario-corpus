## Scenario C1 — C1_Campus_ClassChange

### 1. Overview

- **Scenario ID:** C1  
- **Name:** C1_Campus_ClassChange  
- **Family:** Campus  
- **Settings file:** `corpus_v1/02_campus/C1_Campus_ClassChange.settings`

**Objective**

This scenario models **class change waves on a campus**: students move between buildings every ~50 minutes in a relatively small map, producing periodic increases in contact opportunities. It serves as a campus baseline with **free-space RandomWaypoint mobility** and a **single traffic stream** whose interval is tuned to class-change periods.

### 3. Mobility model

C1 uses **RandomWaypoint** over an 800×600 m campus-like area:

- Nodes move freely in continuous space between uniformly chosen waypoints.  
- Speeds are drawn between **0.8 and 1.5 m/s**, approximating normal walking in a campus environment.  
- Wait times between moves are between **60 and 300 s**, representing time spent in classrooms or rooms before moving to the next class.

There is a single host group (`Group`/`Group1`) with 60 homogeneous nodes and no explicit map or hotspots: the spatial structure comes from the worldSize only.

**DTN implication**

Moderate pedestrian speeds and moderate pause times produce **intermittent contacts** with limited but non-trivial path diversity. The small area increases encounter probability, while the lack of map constraints avoids systematic bottlenecks (contacts emerge mostly from density and random mixing).

### 4. Traffic pattern

Traffic is generated by a **single MessageEventGenerator** (`Events1`):

- `Events.nrof = 1` and `Events1.class = MessageEventGenerator`.  
- **Interval**: `Events1.interval = 2900, 3100` → mean ≈ **3000 s (~50 min)**, matching the comment “Traffic waves every ~50 min (class change intervals)”.  
- **Size**: `Events1.size = 50k, 150k` → mean ≈ **100 kB** messages.  
- **Sources/destinations**: `Events1.hosts = 0, 60` and `Events1.prefix = M` (all nodes can participate; destinations are random as per ONE defaults).

This defines a **moderate, regular traffic stream** aligned with class-change times, without explicit hub-target behaviour or additional event streams.

**DTN implication**

The traffic rate is synchronized with mobility waves, so **forwarding opportunities and offered load co-vary in time**. This tends to create periodic “easy” windows for delivery (during mixing bursts) separated by quieter periods where store-carry-forward dominates.

### 5. Expected network behavior

- **Periodic contact opportunities:** increased mixing around each class-change interval.  
- **High delivery is plausible under Epidemic routing:** moderate density + large buffers reduce drops.  
- **High overhead is expected:** epidemic forwarding replicates aggressively during contact waves, increasing redundant transmissions.  
- **Moderate latency:** many messages can be delivered in the next wave, but messages created just after a wave may wait until the next mixing period.

### 6. Role in the corpus

This scenario is included as a **campus baseline** with:

- moderate density (60 nodes in 800×600 m),
- periodic dynamics (≈50 min rhythm),
- no structural constraints (no map routes, no clusters, no WDM).

It is used as a reference point to compare against more extreme or structured campus scenarios (e.g., exam-day long stays, hackathon, stadium ingress/egress).

### 7. Distinguishing characteristics

Key aspects that distinguish C1 within the campus family:

- **Baseline campus scenario** with **free-space RandomWaypoint**, no map or clusters.  
- Message generation tuned to **class-change periodicity (~50 min)**, producing traffic and contact **waves** over the day.  
- Single homogeneous group of 60 nodes, **short-range radios (10 m)** and **large buffers (50 MB)**, focusing more on temporal structure than on resource stress.  
- Serves as a **reference point** to compare with more extreme campus scenarios (exam day, hackathon, stadium, library, drill).
