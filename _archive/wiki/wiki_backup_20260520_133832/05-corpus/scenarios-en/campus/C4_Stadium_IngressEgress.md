## Scenario C4 — C4_Stadium_IngressEgress

### 1. Overview

- **Scenario ID:** C4  
- **Name:** C4_Stadium_IngressEgress  
- **Family:** Campus  
- **Settings file:** `corpus_v1/02_campus/C4_Stadium_IngressEgress.settings`

**Objective**

This scenario models a **stadium event with ingress and egress waves**: a crowd arrives, mixes during a short pre-event window, then the environment is relatively quiet, and finally a second wave occurs at departure. The key lever is **two time-window traffic peaks** (ingress + egress), intended to create short, intense DTN opportunities separated by a long low-activity period.

### 3. Mobility model

C4 uses **RandomWaypoint** over a 1000×800 m area:

- Speed 0.5–1.2 m/s and short waits (30–180 s) represent crowd movement and brief stops.  
- There is no map routing; movement is free-space mixing in a bounded area.
- The scenario duration is **10,800 s (3 h)**, matching a short event window.

**DTN implication**

With 80 nodes in 0.8 km² and moderate walking speed, the scenario can produce **high contact opportunity** during the event windows. Because movement is unconstrained (no map), connectivity is driven by density and range rather than bottlenecks; this tends to amplify epidemic replication when traffic is present.

### 4. Traffic pattern

Traffic is generated by **two MessageEventGenerators**, creating **two strong bursts**:

- `Events.nrof = 2` with `Events1` and `Events2`.  
- **Ingress burst**: `Events1.time = 0, 900` (first 15 min), `Events1.interval = 5, 15` → mean ≈ 10 s.  
- **Egress burst**: `Events2.time = 6300, 7200` (last 15 min), `Events2.interval = 5, 15` → mean ≈ 10 s.  
- Message size `50k, 150k` → mean ≈ 100 kB; sources/destinations are random among 0..80.
Outside these windows there is effectively no message generation, so the scenario alternates between **high-load burst phases** and a long quiet period.

**DTN implication**

The burst windows align offered load with short, intense mixing periods, which typically yields **high delivery** but also **very high overhead** under flooding: replication explodes when both contacts and traffic are maximal. The quiet gap behaves like a “carry” phase where forwarding depends on nodes moving with no new injections.

### 5. Expected network behavior

- **Two delivery-friendly windows:** ingress and egress periods create intense forwarding opportunities.  
- **Very high delivery is plausible under Epidemic** during the bursts (high density, short waits).  
- **Very high overhead is expected:** two high-rate bursts + epidemic replication amplify redundant transmissions.  
- **Latency depends on window timing:** messages created near a burst can be delivered quickly; messages that survive into the quiet gap may wait until the next burst.

### 6. Role in the corpus

This scenario is included as the **time-window burst campus/crowd** case with:

- a large crowd (N=80) in a moderate area,
- two explicit traffic windows (pattern_burst=1) representing ingress/egress,
- short inter-message times during bursts (≈10 s), and a long quiet gap.

It serves as a reference for **burst-driven load and mixing**, complementary to periodic waves (C1) and long-stay low-mobility regimes (C2/C3).

### 7. Distinguishing characteristics

- **Two explicit traffic peaks** via `Events1.time` and `Events2.time` (ingress + egress).  
- **High instantaneous load** during bursts (≈10 s mean interval) with two generators.  
- Short scenario duration (3 h) representing a single event.  
- Larger crowd (80 nodes) in a 1000×800 m area compared with other campus scenarios.  
- Designed to stress **burst traffic** and **epidemic overhead** more than long-term store-carry-forward.
