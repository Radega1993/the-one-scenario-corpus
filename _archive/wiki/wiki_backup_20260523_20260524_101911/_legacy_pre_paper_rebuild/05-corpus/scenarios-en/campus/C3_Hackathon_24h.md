## Scenario C3 — C3_Hackathon_24h

### 1. Overview

- **Scenario ID:** C3  
- **Name:** C3_Hackathon_24h  
- **Family:** Campus  
- **Settings file:** `corpus_v1/02_campus/C3_Hackathon_24h.settings`

**Objective**

This scenario models a **24-hour hackathon** on a campus: participants stay at desks or in breakout areas for long periods (20–60 min) with very low mobility, and the simulation runs for **24 hours** (86 400 s). It captures **continuous presence**, overnight activity, and local mixing within a single space. Levers are long wait times, very low speed, and extended simulation duration, stressing store-carry-forward over a full day with sparse movement.

### 3. Mobility model

C3 uses **RandomWaypoint** over 800×600 m with **hackathon levers**:

- **Speed** 0.1–0.4 m/s: very slow movement (e.g. between desks, to rest area, back).  
- **Wait time** 1200–3600 s (20–60 min): long stays in one place, mimicking focused work or rest.  
- **Simulation length** 86 400 s (24 h): continuous activity overnight, so contact opportunities and traffic extend across the full day.

Single host group, 40 homogeneous nodes, no map or hotspots.

**DTN implication**

Very low speed and long pauses **strongly limit contact opportunities** and **increase store-carry-forward dominance**. Mixing is local and infrequent; messages may sit in buffers for long periods. The 24 h horizon allows late deliveries that would expire in shorter scenarios, but latency can be high.

### 4. Traffic pattern

Traffic is generated by a **single MessageEventGenerator** (`Events1`):

- **Interval**: `Events1.interval = 120, 300` → mean ≈ **210 s** between messages per source (moderate rate).  
- **Size**: `Events1.size = 50k, 150k` → mean ≈ **100 kB**.  
- **Sources/destinations**: `Events1.hosts = 0, 40` (all nodes can participate; random destinations).

Traffic is **moderate and regular** over the 24 h run; no burst windows or hub targeting. The long TTL (3200) lets messages remain in the network for many hours, so delivery depends on the rare movement phases.

**DTN implication**

Offered load is **moderate** relative to the **very sparse contacts**, so buffer pressure is less extreme than in C2, but delivery still depends on forwarding during the few mixing windows. Long TTL reduces expiry-driven drops and allows late deliveries, at the cost of high mean latency.

### 5. Expected network behavior

- **Sparse, occasional contact opportunities:** long stays and very low speed; mixing happens mainly when nodes move between “sessions”.  
- **Moderate delivery** plausible: long TTL and 24 h run give time for store-carry-forward to deliver, but limited contacts cap throughput.  
- **Overhead** moderate: replication is bounded by few contacts.  
- **High latency** expected: many messages wait a long time for the next encounter, so mean latency can be on the order of thousands of seconds.

### 6. Role in the corpus

This scenario is included as the **24 h hackathon campus variant** with:

- very low mobility (0.1–0.4 m/s, 20–60 min waits),
- **24 h simulation** (distinct from 12 h campus baseline),
- long TTL (3200) and moderate traffic rate.

It is used to compare **extended-duration, low-mobility** behavior against exam-day (C2), library (C7), and class-change (C1), and to stress protocols over a full day of sparse mixing.

### 7. Distinguishing characteristics

- **24 h run** (Scenario.endTime = 86 400 s): only campus scenario with full-day duration.  
- **Very long wait times** (20–60 min) and **very low speed** (0.1–0.4 m/s): hackathon-at-desk semantics.  
- **Long TTL (3200)** so messages can persist across many movement cycles.  
- Same **free-space RandomWaypoint**, single group, single stream as C1/C2; differentiated by duration and extreme pause/speed.
