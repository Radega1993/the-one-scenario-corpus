## Scenario C5 — C5_Library_Quiet

### 1. Overview

- **Scenario ID:** C5  
- **Name:** C5_Library_Quiet  
- **Family:** Campus  
- **Settings file:** `corpus_v1/02_campus/C5_Library_Quiet.settings`

**Objective**

This scenario models a **quiet library** environment: people stay seated for long periods with small, infrequent movements, producing sparse but potentially long contacts. It is a campus low-mobility variant designed to contrast with wave-driven (C1) and burst-driven (C4) campus scenarios, emphasizing store-carry-forward over long stays with moderate traffic.

### 3. Mobility model

C7 uses **RandomWaypoint** over a 550×450 m area with low-mobility levers:

- Speed 0.1–0.3 m/s (slow walking).  
- Wait time 600–1800 s (10–30 min) representing long sitting/studying sessions.

Single host group, no map routing or hotspots.

**DTN implication**

Long pauses reduce encounter frequency, but when contacts happen they can be longer (nodes remain nearby), enabling transfers despite low speed. This regime tends to produce **high latency** and makes delivery depend on rare movement phases and contact persistence.

### 4. Traffic pattern

Traffic is generated by a single `MessageEventGenerator` (`Events1`):

- Interval 40–80 s (mean ≈ 60 s).  
- Size 50k–150k (mean ≈ 100 kB).  
- Sources/destinations random among 42 nodes.

**DTN implication**

Offered load is moderate, but low mobility limits forwarding opportunities. The long TTL allows messages to persist across multiple study/move cycles, so delivery is feasible but often delayed.

### 5. Expected network behavior

- **Sparse contact opportunities:** long stays dominate, so mixing is limited.  
- **Moderate-to-high delivery is plausible with long TTL** if contacts persist, but delivery can still be constrained by low mobility.  
- **Overhead moderate:** replication is bounded by few encounters.  
- **High latency expected:** many messages wait for the next movement/contact episode.

### 6. Role in the corpus

This scenario is included as the **quiet long-stay campus** case with:

- very low mobility (0.1–0.3 m/s, 10–30 min waits),
- moderate traffic (≈60 s mean interval),
- long TTL (6800).

It complements exam-day (C2) and hackathon (C3) by providing a “quiet” long-stay baseline and helps separate long-stay effects from other levers (bursts, periodic waves, directional evacuation).

### 7. Distinguishing characteristics

- Very low mobility with long waits (library semantics).  
- Smaller transmit range (7 m) compared to other campus cases.  
- Long TTL and moderate message rate.  
- Free-space RandomWaypoint, single group (clean baseline for long-stay campus).
